Automatic differentiation(AD) is the core technique in modern deep learning
frameworks. There are two main methods: forward & backward mode AD.

## The Chain Rule

Chain rule is the core principle for both forward & backward AD. The working environment for AD is a calculation DAG, with each node being a tensor $\mathbb{R}^{n}$. Take two node $x$ & $z$, given that there exists a path from $x$ to $z$, then

$$
\begin{align}
\frac{\partial z}{\partial x} = \sum_{i\in\mathrm{pa}(z)} \frac{\partial z}{\partial i}\cdot \frac{\partial i}{\partial x} \\
\frac{\partial z}{\partial x} = \sum_{i\in\mathrm{ch}(x)} \frac{\partial z}{\partial i}\cdot \frac{\partial i}{\partial x}
\end{align}
$$
where $\mathrm{pa}, \mathrm{ch}$ represent direct predecessors & successors of the given node, and $\frac{\partial ~\cdot}{\partial x}$ represents the Jacobian matrix, derivative in the context of tensor calculus.

Next we may apply the principle on the DAG. Take backward mode as an example:

1. Start by calculating $\frac{\partial j}{\partial i}$ for every edge $<i,j>$;
2. Note this would've had all $\frac{\partial z}{\partial i},~i\in \mathrm{pa}(z)$ calculated.
    > You may also think there's a step $\frac{\partial z}{\partial i}=\frac{\partial z}{\partial z} \frac{\partial z}{\partial i}$, where the first item is an identical matrix, for rigor. Whatever.
3. Now for each node $x$ with its $\frac{\partial z}{\partial x}$ not calculated, and $\frac{\partial z}{\partial y}$ calculated for all its children, apply the chain rule. This could be carried out in a (reverse) topological order.
